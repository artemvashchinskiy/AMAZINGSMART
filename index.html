Buying Guide

How to choose a small table lamp or bedside lamp

A good bedside or desk lamp comes down to four things: how you turn it on, how bright it gets, what it's made of, and whether it fits the surface you're putting it on. Here's what to check before you buy.

1. Control type matters more than you'd think

Touch lamps are convenient when you're half-asleep, but they can be unpredictable with smart plugs — check compatibility if you plan to automate your lighting. Inline switches and foot pedals are more reliable for anyone who wants a simple, tactile on/off.

2. Look for a bulb that's already included

Several lamps on this list ship with an LED bulb in the box, which saves a trip to the store and guarantees the color temperature the listing promises. If a lamp doesn't include one, check the socket type (commonly E26 or E27) before ordering a bulb separately.

3. Match the base material to the room

Wood and ceramic bases tend to read warmer and suit bedrooms and living rooms, while metal bases hold up better in a busy home office or a kid's room where a knock or two is inevitable.

4. Measure before you buy

A 17-inch lamp can dominate a narrow nightstand, while a 9-inch lamp might look lost on a wide console table. Check the product dimensions against your actual furniture, not just the photos.

FAQ

Common questions about small lamps

What's the difference between a touch lamp and a dimmable lamp?

All touch lamps are a type of dimmable lamp, but not all dimmable lamps use touch controls — some use a rotary knob, a remote, or a rocker switch instead. Touch lamps typically offer 3 preset brightness levels, while stepless-dimming lamps let you land anywhere on the brightness range.

Is it safe to use a bedside lamp with a smart plug?

Most inline-switch and pull-chain lamps work fine with smart plugs. Touch-activated lamps are more hit-or-miss, since the plug cutting power can reset the lamp's touch sensor — check the individual listing for smart plug compatibility.

What bulb size fits most small table lamps?

E26 and E27 are the two most common bases on this list — they're nearly interchangeable in North America and Europe respectively. Always confirm the maximum wattage listed on the lamp before installing a bulb.
